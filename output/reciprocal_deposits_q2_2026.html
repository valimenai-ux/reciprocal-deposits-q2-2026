Notes

Source definitions

  • Reciprocal deposits (call report). Schedule RC-E, Memorandum item 1.g "Total reciprocal deposits (as of the report date)", MDRM RCONJH83, from the FFIEC CDR bulk download "Call Reports -- Single Period" for 06/30/2026. Reciprocal deposits are deposits a bank receives through a deposit placement network in return for placing the same amount of its own customers' deposits at other network banks (section 29(i) of the FDI Act). Point in time, bank level, USD thousands as filed; summed to the holding company.
  • Brokered reciprocal deposits (call report). Schedule RC-O, item 9 "Reciprocal brokered deposits (included in Schedule RC-E, Memorandum item 1.b)", MDRM RCONG803: the part of reciprocal deposits that the bank reports as brokered because it exceeds, or the bank does not qualify for, the limited exception in 12 CFR 337.6(e). Item 9.a "Fully consolidated reciprocal brokered deposits" (RCONL190) is completed only by institutions that consolidate another insured depository institution; none of the banks in this set report it, so it is shown as not applicable rather than zero.
  • Total brokered deposits (call report). Schedule RC-E, Memorandum item 1.b "Total brokered deposits", MDRM RCON2365. Shown for context only.
  • Total deposits and total assets (call report). Total deposits = Schedule RC item 13.a deposits in domestic offices (RCON2200) plus, for FFIEC 031 filers, item 13.b deposits in foreign offices, Edge and Agreement subsidiaries and IBFs (RCFN2200). Domestic-office deposits are also carried separately. Total assets = Schedule RC item 12 (RCFD2170 for FFIEC 031 filers, RCON2170 otherwise).
  • NSFR sweep/reciprocal/brokered (row 7). Row 7 of the US NSFR public disclosure template (12 CFR 249.131(c)(1)(vii)): "Sweep deposits, brokered reciprocal deposits, and brokered deposits provided by a retail customer or counterparty". Average unweighted amounts by residual maturity (open maturity, < 6 months, 6 months to < 1 year, >= 1 year) and the average weighted amount, for the quarters ended March 31, 2026 and June 30, 2026, taken from each holding company's semiannual NSFR disclosure PDF (file name and page number recorded in nsfr_detail). USD millions.

NSFR bundling caveat

  • Row 7 is a bundle - not a reciprocal number. The NSFR line bundles three things: affiliate and non-affiliate sweep deposits, brokered reciprocal deposits, and other brokered deposits provided by retail customers. Most of it at wealth-management firms is sweep balances. It is labelled "NSFR sweep/reciprocal/brokered (row 7)" everywhere in this repo and must never be read as reciprocal deposits. It also sits on a different basis (consolidated holding company, quarterly average) from the call report figure (insured banks, period end).

Point in time vs quarterly average

  • Call report (point in time) vs NSFR (quarterly average). Call report items are balances as of the 6/30/2026 report date. NSFR disclosure amounts are averages of daily (or, for some firms, more frequent) calculations over the quarter. The two are not directly comparable and are shown side by side only as context; no ratio in this repo mixes a call-report numerator with an NSFR denominator or vice versa.
  • Image-only tables and OCR. Morgan Stanley, HSBC North America and DWS USA embed their NSFR tables as images. Those pages were OCR'd with tesseract in five resolution/segmentation variants that vote, and every figure used was then compared with the rendered page image during the build (row 4, row 7, row 15 and row 40 for Morgan Stanley and HSBC). DWS USA's template omits the retail-funding rows altogether (the firm has no retail funding), so its row 7 is recorded as nil; its total ASF comes from the text summary table on page 5 and its NSFR from the page-8 table.

Scope

  • Holding company vs bank level. Call report figures are summed over every active FDIC-insured depository institution whose regulatory top holder (FDIC BankFind field RSSDHCR) is the holding company. For the foreign-owned firms the top holder in FDIC data is the foreign parent (e.g. Barclays PLC, Deutsche Bank AG, The Toronto-Dominion Bank, UBS Group AG, Bank of Montreal, HSBC Holdings plc, Royal Bank of Canada); their US insured banks are attributed to the US intermediate holding company named in the task. Non-bank affiliates and uninsured trust companies are outside the call report figures. NSFR disclosures are consolidated at the (intermediate) holding company and include non-bank subsidiaries.
  • Structure verified via FDIC BankFind rather than NIC. The subsidiary list and RSSD IDs were verified against the FDIC BankFind Suite institutions API (fields FED_RSSD, RSSDHCR, NAMEHCR; index of 23 October 2026), not against the FFIEC National Information Center, because NIC serves a CAPTCHA challenge to automated clients. The two draw on the same Federal Reserve structure data, but NIC itself was not consulted.

Confidentiality change

  • RC-O items 9 and 9.a become confidential from the 9/30/2026 report date. The Federal Reserve MDRM data dictionary (downloaded 27 October 2026) carries two entries for RCONG803 and RCONL190: public (Confidentiality = N) through the 6/30/2026 report date and confidential (Confidentiality = Y) from 9/30/2026 onward. Schedule RC-E Memorandum item 1.g (RCONJH83) stays public. The 6/30/2026 report is therefore the last public quarter for the brokered-reciprocal split used here.
  • Call Report instruction changes behind the confidentiality of RC-O items 9 and 9.a. The FFIEC Supplemental Instructions for September 2026 (transmitted by FDIC FIL-62-2026, October 5, 2026) state: "Consistent with the IFR, brokered reciprocal deposits reported in Schedule RC-O, item 9 and, if applicable, item 9.a will be confidential and will no longer be made available to the public on an individual institution basis." The agencies' Paperwork Reduction Act notice (91 FR 67650, October 27, 2026) gives the rationale: the amounts, compared with public total reciprocal deposits in RC-E Memorandum item 1.g, could reveal nonpublic supervisory information (a bank that stops qualifying as an agent institution must report all reciprocal deposits as brokered). The change took effect with the September 30, 2026 report date under an OMB emergency clearance of September 28, 2026. Copies: data/raw/reference/.

FDIC interim final rule (Aug 2026)

  • FDIC interim final rule raising the reciprocal deposit cap. On August 27, 2026 the FDIC Board approved the interim final rule "Reciprocal Deposits: Implementing the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act" (91 FR 56022, September 1, 2026; RIN 3064-AG32; effective September 1, 2026; comments were due October 1, 2026). It conforms 12 CFR 337.6(e) to section 902 of Public Law 119-101 (enacted July 11, 2026), which replaced the old general cap on reciprocal deposits excluded from brokered deposits (the lesser of $5 billion or 20% of total liabilities) with a tiered calculation: 50% of total liabilities up to $1 billion, plus 40% of the portion between $1 billion and $10 billion, plus 30% of the portion between $10 billion and $96.33 billion, so the cap reaches a maximum of $30 billion for institutions with total liabilities of $96.33 billion or more (the rule's own example: $25 billion of liabilities gives a cap of $8.6 billion). It also widens the agent-institution definition to well-capitalized banks with a CAMELS composite rating of 1, 2 or 3 (previously 1 or 2) and clarifies requalification and the receipt of nonmaturity reciprocal deposits. The 6/30/2026 Call Report figures in this repo predate the rule: brokered reciprocal amounts (RC-O item 9) were still determined under the old cap. Sources: Federal Register PDF (data/raw/reference/FR_2026-09-01_FDIC_interim_final_rule_reciprocal_deposits_91FR56022.pdf), FDIC FIL-52-2026, Public Law 119-101 (data/raw/reference/).

Open items and judgment calls

  1. Structure source. Subsidiary lists and RSSD IDs were verified against the FDIC BankFind Suite institutions API (fields FED_RSSD, RSSDHCR, NAMEHCR; data index of 23 October 2026), not the FFIEC NIC, because NIC (www.ffiec.gov) serves a Cloudflare CAPTCHA to automated clients. Both draw on Federal Reserve structure data, but NIC itself was not consulted. The FDIC list reflects active institutions at the time of the pull (late October 2026), not exactly 6/30/2026; all 39 banks have 6/30/2026 Call Reports in the CDR file.
  2. IHC attribution. For foreign-owned firms the FDIC top holder is the foreign parent (Barclays PLC, Deutsche Bank AG, The Toronto-Dominion Bank, UBS Group AG, Bank of Montreal, HSBC Holdings plc, Royal Bank of Canada). Their US insured banks are attributed to the US (intermediate) holding company named in the brief; the attribution of the smaller banks (RBC Bank (Georgia) N.A., Deutsche Bank Trust Company Delaware, HSBC Trust Company (Delaware) N.A., BMO Harris Central N.A., TD Bank USA N.A.) to the IHC is inferred from the parent, not read from NIC.
  3. Subsidiaries beyond the brief. FDIC data adds insured subsidiaries the brief did not list, all included in the rollups: Bank of America California N.A.; JPMorgan Chase Bank, Dearborn; Wells Fargo National Bank West and Wells Fargo Bank South Central N.A.; BNY Mellon N.A., The Bank of New York Mellon Trust Company N.A. and BNY Mellon Trust of Delaware; Deutsche Bank Trust Company Delaware; TD Bank USA N.A.; BMO Harris Central N.A.; HSBC Trust Company (Delaware) N.A.; RBC Bank (Georgia) N.A. All report zero reciprocal deposits except none; they add deposits to the denominators. DWS USA Corporation has no insured depository subsidiary.
  4. NSFR disclosures not retrievable. Seven firms publish a Q1-Q2 2026 NSFR disclosure that could not be downloaded by an automated client (JPMorgan Chase, Capital One, Charles Schwab, U.S. Bancorp, American Express: HTTP 403 bot protection on the IR host; Northern Trust and BMO: connection closed without a response). Their NSFR columns are blank. URLs checked are in data/processed/sources.csv. Manual fix: download in a browser to data/raw/nsfr/<slug>_nsfr_2026q2.pdf, set status=found, re-run scripts 04-07.
  5. NSFR not required / not filed. Fifth Third (Category IV in Q2 2026; Category III from Q3 2026) and Huntington (Category IV) had no NSFR requirement and publish nothing. RBC US Group Holdings LLC appears as Category III in the Federal Reserve's June 2026 listing (Category IV in 2025) but has published no NSFR disclosure; under the rule's transition timing none is expected for Q2 2026. Nothing was estimated for these firms.
  6. Category corrections. BMO Financial Corp. is Category III (85% NSFR), not Category IV; Charles Schwab and TD Group US Holdings run the full 100% NSFR; American Express became Category II during Q2 2026 with an 85% transition factor; RBC US moved to Category III. The brief's labels are kept in the 'category' column and the verified values in data/reference/holding_companies.csv (category_verified).
  7. RC-O item 9.a. Schedule RC-O item 9.a (RCONL190, 'Fully consolidated reciprocal brokered deposits') is completed only by institutions that consolidate another insured depository institution; none of the 39 banks report it (12 banks in the whole population do, all zero). It is shown as not applicable, not as zero.
  8. Pre-rule call report. The 6/30/2026 Call Report precedes the FDIC interim final rule of September 1, 2026 (tiered cap up to $30bn) and the ROAD to Housing Act of July 11, 2026; RC-O item 9 amounts reflect the old cap (lesser of $5bn or 20% of liabilities). From the 9/30/2026 report date RC-O items 9 and 9.a are confidential, so this is the last public quarter for the brokered-reciprocal split.
  9. Zero reciprocal balances. 11 of the 24 holding companies with insured banks report zero reciprocal deposits in RC-E Memorandum item 1.g (including JPMorgan Chase, Citigroup, Wells Fargo, Goldman Sachs, State Street, Northern Trust, American Express, Charles Schwab, Barclays US, TD Group US, BMO). These are reported values, not missing data.
  10. OCR-derived NSFR figures. Morgan Stanley, HSBC North America and DWS USA publish image-only tables. Figures were OCR'd (five variants voting) and then checked visually against the rendered page images; DWS USA's template omits the retail rows, so its row 7 is nil by construction. HSBC's Q2 2026 ratio is taken from table row 40 (129.3%).
  11. Data vintage. The CDR bulk file is the FFIEC's 'as of 2026-10-15' extract (see data/raw/call_reports/Readme.txt); amendments filed after that date are not reflected. NSFR PDFs were downloaded on 27 October 2026 and their SHA-256 hashes are recorded in data/processed/sources.csv.
  12. Units and rounding. Call Report items are in USD thousands as filed; NSFR amounts are in USD millions as published (DWS USA to two decimals). The dashboard shows $bn to two decimals and percentages to two decimals; the spreadsheet carries the full values.
